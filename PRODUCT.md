# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Homeowners and landlords in Southampton and surrounding postcodes (SO14–SO51) who need a plumber or gas engineer. They are typically:

- Searching on a phone, often urgently (no hot water, leak, broken boiler) — high-stress, low-patience context.
- Comparing two or three local trades quickly. They want to feel they've found someone who is qualified, reachable, fair, and not going to mess them around.
- Mostly non-technical. They care about: "is this a real person", "is he Gas Safe", "what will it cost", "can he come today or tomorrow", "where is he based".

The job-to-be-done: get a trustworthy local plumber booked or in conversation in under two minutes, on a phone, without filling in a long form.

## Product Purpose

Better Call Wes is a sole-trader plumbing and heating business. The website's job is to convert local search and ad traffic into WhatsApp messages, phone calls, or online bookings. Success looks like:

- A first-time visitor lands on a page (often a location page from organic search or a paid ad), instantly understands they're in the right place (right area, right trade, Gas Safe), and can take action with one tap.
- The site reinforces credibility cues that matter for a one-man trade business: real face, real van, Gas Safe number on the website (not in ads), local postcodes, transparent pricing where possible, real customer reviews.
- Visitors do not bounce because the page looks generic, AI-generated, template-y, or like a national lead-gen middleman.

## Brand Personality

Three-word personality: **trusted, local, no-nonsense**.

- Voice: plain British English. Direct. Never corporate. Never salesy. Sounds like a competent tradesperson who happens to write clearly, not a marketing department.
- Tone: confident but not loud. Friendly but not chummy. Honest about what's covered and what isn't.
- Emotional goal: relief. The visitor lands on the page worried about their problem and leaves feeling "sorted, this guy will sort it."

## Anti-references

This site should NOT look or feel like:

- **National lead-gen aggregators** (Checkatrade-style template pages, Bark, RatedPeople listing-style layouts). They feel impersonal and generic; visitors associate them with bait-and-switch.
- **Gradient-text, glassmorphism, hero-metric SaaS landing pages.** Wrong register entirely. A trade site that looks like a B2B SaaS product loses trust instantly.
- **Stock-photo plumber sites.** Smiling man in branded polo shirt, thumbs up, white teeth, generic kitchen. Anti-trust signal. Use real photos of Wes, the real van, real jobs.
- **Cookie-cutter trade-website templates** (e.g. Wix/Squarespace plumber templates with the same hero layout, same "Why Choose Us" four-icon row, same blue-and-orange palette). The category-reflex aesthetic this site must avoid.
- **Em dashes, AI-style copy ("In today's fast-paced world..."), buzzwords ("solutions", "synergies", "leverage").** Reads as AI slop and undermines the trust register.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the person, not the brand.** A real face and a real van outpull a logo every time on a one-man trade site. Imagery should look like a phone photo of an actual job, not a stock library.
2. **One thumb, one decision.** Every page must have an obvious primary action (WhatsApp or call) reachable with the right thumb on a phone, above the fold, at the bottom, and sticky if needed. Never ask the visitor to choose between five things.
3. **Local first.** Postcode, area name, and physical placeness should be visible everywhere. A Southampton homeowner should never wonder "is this guy actually local to me." This beats generic copy on every page.
4. **Earn the credibility before asking for the click.** Gas Safe (visible on site, not in ads), real reviews with names, transparent pricing or honest "from £X" where appropriate, and the actual postcodes covered. No badges that aren't real.
5. **Plain over polished.** This is not the place for delight, easter eggs, animations, or visual flourish. A page that looks slightly under-designed but obviously real beats a page that looks expensively designed but generic.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.1 AA. The site is read by older homeowners and by people in stressful situations who may be reading on a cracked phone screen in a leaking kitchen — generous type sizes, real contrast (the navy / orange / white palette already supports this if applied correctly), and tap targets sized for thumbs (44px minimum).
- Reduced-motion users must get a static experience. No essential information conveyed only through animation or hover.
- Phone number, WhatsApp link, and address must be readable to screen readers and selectable as text (not images, not background-image-only).
- British English throughout. Currency in £.
